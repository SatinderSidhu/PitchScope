Getting started

Click Enable microphone, allow access, and sing.

The browser asks for the microphone once. Say yes and the permission holds for the whole session — pressing Stop pauses the analysis but keeps the microphone open on purpose, so you are never asked twice. On the published https site the browser usually remembers the answer permanently.

Everything after that is optional setup: where your Sa sits, which script you want the notes named in, and the tempo you are practising against. All of it is remembered for next time.

Reading the screen

The big note and the cents meter

The large label is the note you are singing right now, with the other naming systems underneath it. Below that, the number in cents is how far off you are from that note being exactly in tune. A cent is a hundredth of a semitone; 100 cents is the gap between two adjacent piano keys.

ReadingMeaning
under 12¢In tune. Most listeners cannot hear this as wrong.
12–30¢Audibly drifting. Worth correcting.
over 30¢Clearly off — a third of the way to the next note.

The meter needle shows the same thing as a position: centred is in tune, left is flat, right is sharp.

The pitch timeline

The scrolling graph is the heart of it. Each row is one semitone, labelled on the left. Time runs left to right, newest at the playhead.

  • Blocks are the notes you held, coloured by how in tune they were.
  • The thin white line on top is your actual pitch, drawn continuously. This is where scoops into a note, vibrato and slow drift become visible — the block says which note, the line says how you got there and whether you stayed.
  • Vertical lines are beats; the thicker ones are bar lines.

The beat lane

Under the graph, one cell per beat showing which note occupied that beat. This is the rhythm read-out: what landed on beat 1, 2, 3, 4.

Recent notes and Accuracy by note

Every note you sing is listed, newest first, with a ±50¢ bar. The solid tick is where the note averaged out; the translucent band behind it is how far the pitch wandered while you held it — a wide band means an unsteady note even when the average looks fine.

Accuracy by note is the same data averaged per note and sorted worst first, needing at least two attempts before a note appears. This is the practice list: it turns one bad attempt into nothing, and a habit into an obvious line at the top.

Sa and sargam

Sa is your tonic. Everything in the Indian naming is measured from it.

Set it with the SA note and octave selectors. B2 is the default. Move Sa and every swara label across the whole app moves with it — the keyboards, the graph, the history, the notation.

Labels

The LABELS menu picks the script and how much to show. Any of Punjabi, Hindi, Urdu or roman sargam, on its own or with the western note alongside it. It only changes what is written — never what is measured.

Scale and thaat

Tells the app which notes belong to your tune, and dims the rest.

Rows on the graph that are outside the chosen scale are darkened, so straying outside it is visible at a glance. That is all it does: it never filters, snaps or corrects your pitch. Sing anything and it is still measured and recorded exactly as sung.

The ten thaats are the parent scales of Hindustani music — each fixes which of the twelve swaras are shuddha, komal or teevra, and every raag sits inside one. Kafi, for example, is Sa Re ♭Ga Ma Pa Dha ♭Ni.

chromatic (all 12) dims nothing — right when you are working out a new tune and do not yet know its scale. The major and natural minor entries are the same idea under western names; they duplicate Bilawal and Asavari.

Everything is rooted on your Sa, so moving Sa moves the whole scale with it. The scale is saved with each take, so replaying an old take restores what you sang it against.

Auto range and flats

AUTO RANGE

Controls how much of the pitch span the graph and keyboards cover. On, the window fits itself to what you have sung in the last twenty seconds, with a little headroom and a two-octave minimum. Off, it stays where you put it with shift + scroll on the graph, or a two-finger pinch on a phone.

Turn it off when the rescaling distracts you mid-phrase, or when you want two takes framed identically so they can be compared. Zooming manually switches it off for you.

FLATS

Purely how western names are spelled: off gives C♯ D♯ F♯ G♯ A♯, on gives D♭ E♭ G♭ A♭ B♭. Same pitches, different label. It does not touch sargam, which carries its own accidentals.

It is a blunt global toggle, not key-aware — it will not pick the theoretically correct spelling for a given key. With Sa on B, sharps are the correct spelling, so leaving it off is right.

Playing the keyboards

Both keyboards are playable, so you always have a reference pitch.

There are two: a vertical rail down the left of the graph, which doubles as its axis so one row on the graph is the same height as its key, and the familiar wide keyboard along the bottom.

  • Click or tap a key to hear it. Hold to sustain, drag or slide to glide between notes. On a touch screen several fingers work at once.
  • SUSTAIN latches instead: tap a key to start it, tap again to stop. Latch Sa and Pa together and you have a drone to sing against.
  • KEY SOUND turns the clicks off entirely, VOL sets the level, and Esc stops every sounding note.

Keys are coloured by what they mean: violet is a key you pressed, while green, amber and red show how in tune the note you sang was.

Use headphones. Through speakers the microphone hears the reference tone as well as your voice, and the app will happily track the tone instead of you.

Tempo, meter and metronome

♩= sets the tempo and METER the beats per bar — 4/4 by default, with 3/4, 2/4, 5/4, 6/8 and 7/8 available. Together they draw the beat and bar lines on the graph, fill the beat lane, and decide how the notation is barred.

METRONOME plays a click, higher on the downbeat. It is deliberately not restored when you reload: a page cannot start making noise before you have interacted with it.

A4 is the reference pitch everything is measured against, 440 Hz by default. Change it to match an instrument tuned differently, and every note and frequency in the app moves with it.

Recording takes

Press ⏺ Rec, sing, press it again. The take is saved on your own machine.

A take stores two things: the audio, and the pitch analysis. ☰ Takes lists them with date, length and size.

  • Replay puts the whole app into review: the graph, both keyboards, the note history and the accuracy list all replay in sync with the recording, so you can hear a phrase and watch exactly where it drifted.
  • Rename — or just click the take's name — to edit it in place. Enter saves, Esc cancels.
  • Delete removes it permanently.

Each take also remembers the setup it was sung under — Sa, script, scale, tempo, meter, A4 — and restores it on replay, so an old take is never relabelled by whatever you have changed since.

Keep the tab in front while recording. Browsers stop animation frames in a background tab, which pauses the pitch analysis even though the audio keeps recording. The app warns you if this happens.

Clear is refused during a take, because restarting the clock mid-take would leave the audio and the pitch track disagreeing about where time went. Pausing with Stop is safe.

Notation for a musician

♪ Notes writes out what you sang as something another person can play.

It transcribes whatever is on screen — the live session, or the take being replayed. Held notes are snapped to the beat, grouped into bars of your taal, and drawn as sargam with the western name under each swara, so the same sheet works for a harmonium player or a guitarist.

Live session — 6 notes
Sa = B2 (123.5 Hz, A4 = 440)
Taal 4/4 at 80 bpm
Each bar is one line; – holds the previous note, · is a rest

  1 | ਸਾ   –    | ਰੇ   –    | ਗਾ   –    | ਮਾ   –    |
  2 | ਪਾ   –    | –    –    | ·    ·    | ਸਾ'  –    |

The header matters as much as the notes: without knowing where your Sa sits in western terms and in Hz, the sargam is unplayable by anyone but you.

GRID chooses 1, 2 or 4 subdivisions per beat. Raise it for fast phrases, lower it when ornaments clutter the sheet.

Three ways to hand it over: Copy text, Download .txt, and Download .mid — a standard MIDI file that any notation app, DAW or keyboard will open. That last one is usually most useful, since your musician can hear the tune rather than only read it.

Two limits worth knowing. Quantising uses the tempo you have set, so if you sang freely against a different pulse the bars will not line up — set ♩= to match first. And a meend or glide becomes discrete notes, because a note grid cannot express a continuous slide. The pitch graph still shows the real gesture.

How accurate is it

Pitch is found with the McLeod Pitch Method — an autocorrelation technique rather than spectral peak-picking, chosen because a sung vowel often has a weak or missing fundamental that makes spectral methods report a note an octave too high. A running median over recent frames removes the isolated octave flips that survive that, and a clarity gate suppresses breath noise.

Measured against synthesised tones from E2 to G5, the readout lands within a third of a cent. Your own accuracy will be limited by room noise and microphone quality long before the algorithm is.

The reference tones the keyboards play are built from two oscillators detuned symmetrically, so the pair beats gently but the pitch itself stays exact — a one-sided detune would leave every reference note a couple of cents sharp, which is no good in a tool whose job is telling you that you are two cents sharp.

Privacy and storage

Nothing you sing is ever uploaded. There is no server.

All the analysis happens in the browser on your own machine. Recorded takes are kept in your browser's IndexedDB — chosen over localStorage, which caps out near 5 MB and holds only text, while a minute of audio is around a megabyte. The Takes panel shows how much room you are using.

Settings live in localStorage. Both are per-browser and per-device: takes recorded on your laptop will not appear on your phone, and deleting a take deletes it for good.

Troubleshooting

No note appears while I sing

Check the meter shows a device and that Stop is showing (which means it is running). Confidence under about 70% means the app is not sure enough to commit — usually too quiet, too breathy, or too much room noise. Sing a sustained vowel rather than a consonant.

The note jumps an octave

Very breathy or very quiet singing can do this. Getting closer to the microphone usually fixes it.

It keeps asking for the microphone

It should ask once per session. If you are on http://localhost, the browser forgets between page loads; the published https site remembers. Safari re-asks on every load by design.

My take has no audio

The record audio checkbox may be off, or the browser may not support recording. The pitch track is saved either way, so the replay still works — the take is simply labelled "no audio".

Fullscreen does nothing

Some embedded browser views refuse fullscreen; the app will tell you when the request is denied. iPhone Safari has no fullscreen at all — add the page to your home screen instead and it runs without browser chrome.

The notation does not line up with what I sang

Set ♩= to the tempo you actually sang at before transcribing. The bars are drawn from that number, not from your performance.
